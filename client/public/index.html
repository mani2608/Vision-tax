Help & FAQs

Frequently Asked Questions

Everything you need to know about US tax filing, visa status, and refunds.

Anyone who earned taxable U.S. income above the IRS gross filing threshold ($14,600 for Single filers) or is required to report visa status (such as international students submitting Form 8843) must file. Filing rules depend on visa category (F-1, H-1B, L-1, B-1), residency status (Resident vs Non-Resident Alien), and income sources.

Form 1040 is filed by U.S. citizens, green card holders, and resident aliens who pass the Substantial Presence Test. Form 1040-NR is for non-resident aliens (such as international students on F-1 during their first 5 calendar years). Non-residents are taxed only on U.S.-sourced income and follow distinct deduction rules.

Yes! Non-resident students on F-1 and J-1 visas are exempt from FICA taxes under Internal Revenue Code Section 3121(b)(19). If your employer withheld FICA taxes in error, we help you file Form 843 and Form 8316 to recover 100% of the funds from the IRS.

Yes. If you have financial accounts in India or other foreign countries exceeding $10,000 at any time during the year, you are legally required to file an FBAR. We handle both FBAR and FATCA disclosures accurately to protect you from severe IRS penalties.

The IRS typically issues direct deposit refunds within 21 days for electronically filed returns without errors. Paper returns or returns requiring identity verification may take 6 to 8 weeks. You can track your real-time status on our Refund Status page.
